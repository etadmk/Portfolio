01 · Why the boom gets hot on one side

A tube in sunlight is a two-node radiation problem with a weak conduction link around the circumference.

hot half absorbs αS × 2R per unit length each half radiates εσT⁴ × πR circumferential 4 k t ΔT / (πR) bare CFRP, k_transverse ≈ 2 W/mK → ΔT ≈ 190 K with a thin high-k wrap or liner → ΔT ≈ 70 K

The counterintuitive bit worth saying out loud: carbon composite is the good material here despite conducting badly. Its transverse conductivity is poor, so it holds a bigger gradient than aluminium would. It wins anyway, and the next section is why.

02 · The gradient bends the boom

This is the number they want. Two lines of geometry.

That is the answer to "why composite booms." Not stiffness-to-weight — near-zero CTE. An aluminium boom bows almost a metre at the tip; a tailored layup bows a centimetre. Then you add a thin aluminium liner or a pitch-fibre wrap purely to short the circumference thermally, and you get another factor of three.

03 · Thermal cycling — snap and flutter

The second half of their question. Know the dimensionless group and know which regime you are in.

thermal time constant τ_T = ρct / (4εσT³) CFRP, 0.5 mm, 300 K: 720 / 5.2 ≈ 140 s structural period T_s = 1/f₁ 10 m cantilever tube, f₁ ≈ 1.3 Hz → 0.8 s Boley number B = τ_T / T_s ≈ 180

For the bare boom, thermal response is two minutes and the structure rings in under a second, so heating cannot pump the mode — you get slow bowing, not flutter. Say the caveat unprompted: hang a large membrane off four booms and the system's fundamental frequency collapses toward a fraction of a hertz, which moves you left on that line. The vehicle-level number is the one that matters, not the boom's.

Separately from flutter there is thermal snap — the transient at eclipse entry and exit. The shadow edge sweeps past in seconds, the boom's curvature changes over a couple of minutes, and any sudden release of stored strain (a joint slipping, a wrinkle popping) shows up as an impulse. It is the mechanism behind the Hubble solar array jitter, and it is a good thing to name by that history.

04 · Four booms make it asymmetric

The observation that will separate you from a candidate who only answered the single-boom question.

Three consequences to name:

  • The structure is statically indeterminate. Thermal strain in one boom is reacted by the other three through the membrane, so you cannot analyse a boom alone — you need the assembly.
  • Figure error becomes time-varying. As the vehicle slews during a service pass, the sun angle on each boom changes, so the membrane shape drifts while you are trying to point.
  • Centre of pressure moves. An asymmetrically bowed reflector shifts CP relative to CM, which turns solar radiation pressure into a growing disturbance torque. Thermal distortion feeds the attitude control problem.

The answer that shows judgment
Do not reach for a heat pipe. The gradient you are fixing spans 100 mm of circumference — a few grams of graphite wrap or a thin aluminium liner solves it passively with no fluid, no charge, no leak path and no ground-test orientation constraint. Heat pipes earn their mass moving watts over metres, not kelvins across a tube. Saying "this is a conduction problem, not a two-phase problem" is a better answer than any heat pipe design.

06 · Heat pipes — reusable reference

This section is the same for Reflect, Cowboy Space and anyone else. Learn it once.

Δp_capillary ≥ Δp_liquid + Δp_vapour + Δp_gravity Δp_cap,max = 2σcosθ / r_c five limits: capillary · boiling · entrainment · sonic · viscous capacity quoted as QL in watt-metres — heat × transport distance
ConsiderationWhat to say
Working fluidAmmonia for 200–400 K, propylene colder, methanol lower performance. Figure of merit M = ρσh_fg/μ. Water has the best merit number and is unusable because it freezes at 273 K.
FreezingAmmonia freezes at 195 K. A pipe with a cold condenser can freeze, and frozen startup is a genuine failure mode — vapour flows to a blocked condenser and the evaporator dries out. Heaters or a warm parking orientation.
Ground testGrooved pipes are sensitive to fractions of a degree of tilt. Test level, or with the evaporator high, or reflux flatters the result and you fly an unverified article.
Crossing a hingeA rigid CCHP cannot cross a deployment joint. That is where a loop heat pipe earns its complexity — smooth flexible transport tubing with the wick confined to the evaporator.
NCGNon-condensable gas migrates to the coldest end and blocks condenser area. Deliberately exploited in a variable conductance heat pipe with a gas reservoir for passive temperature control.
Boiling limitNucleation inside the wick rather than at the meniscus blocks the liquid return path. Distinct from capillary dryout and worse in microgravity where bubbles do not float clear.
AlternativesPyrolytic graphite sheet reaches roughly 1000–1900 W/mK in-plane at low density and is flexible. For short paths it beats a heat pipe on mass and has no fluid. Always price this option first.

07 · Materials and structures — reusable reference

TopicWhat to say
Local shell bucklingσ_cr ≈ 0.6 E t/R for a perfect cylinder, but imperfection knockdown factors of 0.2–0.5 are standard. Thin tubes fail locally before they fail globally.
Bow reduces capacityA thermally bowed boom carries its axial load eccentrically, so the P–δ effect eats buckling margin. Thermal and structural cases are coupled, not sequential.
Open sectionsSTEM and TRAC booms roll flat for stowage but have very poor torsional stiffness, because an open thin-walled section has almost no St Venant J.
Stowage creepA boom rolled for months takes a viscoelastic set and deploys to the wrong shape. Drives material choice, stow duration and sometimes a thermal reset after deployment.
MoistureComposites absorb water on the ground and release it in vacuum. The resulting dimensional change can rival the thermal strain. Bake out before launch and account for the shift.
MicrocrackingThermal cycling cracks the matrix, which changes both CTE and stiffness over life. A qualification campaign has to cycle, not just cold-soak.
Fastened jointsPreload prevents gapping and slip; it is lost to embedment, relaxation and CTE mismatch. Nothing is retorqued on orbit, so every fastener needs a locking feature. NASA-STD-5020 is the reference.
