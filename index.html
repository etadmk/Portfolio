Marine energy · off-grid power systems

Running instruments on a battery the sun only sometimes refills

A year spent on one question: how do you keep a scientific instrument working in the ocean when its entire power budget depends on the weather, and nobody can reach the hardware. The answer turned out to be less about better forecasting and more about telling a healthy system apart from a broken one using telemetry alone.

Duty Cycle Planner

Give it a site, a battery and a list of instruments in priority order. It allocates the available power, simulates the battery forward hour by hour, and cuts duty cycles back rather than let the charge cross the safety floor. Defaults are the measured buoy.

Shows: energy-budget modelling, constrained allocation, and the difference between a calculator and a scheduler, which is that a scheduler is allowed to say no.

Investigation

Panel Loss, Revisited

A buoy appeared to be losing about 60% of its solar output. That reading held for weeks and shaped the project's conclusions. It was wrong: the panel was fine and the charge controller was deliberately backing off a full battery. Hiding inside the same window was a real eight-day outage that had never logged a fault code.

The same method was then run against a second site on hardware I had never touched, and reproduced.

Shows: diagnostic reasoning under uncertainty, and a willingness to overturn my own published finding rather than let it stand.

How to read this

Scope, stated plainly: this work was telemetry-only by necessity. The buoy came out of the water partway through the fellowship and I never had hands on either system. Every diagnosis here was made at a distance and then confirmed by someone who could see the hardware.

That turned out to be the interesting constraint rather than a limiting one. It is the same position anyone is in when an instrument is two hundred miles offshore, and it is where the actual finding came from: remote diagnosis gets you a short list of candidates, not an answer. Knowing precisely where that list ends, and what has to happen next, is the useful part.
