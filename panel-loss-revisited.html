Panel loss, revisited

Aug 14 – Sep 2, 2026

Three weeks ago the headline finding was that panel health matters more than forecast accuracy — built on a reading of ~60% output loss starting June 4 at the Gulf Coast buoy. That reading turned out to be wrong. The same curtailment-vs-fault method was then run a second time, against a newer Hawaii Island site, and closed out cleanly there. Here's both threads, and what's left.

01 Gulf Coast — the panel-loss reversal

Aug 14

The ~60% loss goes out for hardware review unconfirmed

The panel-output drop from June 4 onward was the basis for the entire "panel health > forecasting" argument — but it was inferred from satellite irradiance vs. telemetry, never checked against the actual charge controller. Flagged as the one number the whole finding rested on.

Aug 18

Ruled out: it isn't degradation confirmed

Cross-checked against the controller's own state codes. The battery reaches full every day and the controller intentionally backs off — Bulk → Absorption → Float, on a daily cycle. The panel was never failing; the system was doing exactly what it's supposed to do.

Aug 18

A real outage turns up instead open

Same review surfaced something the original story missed: from June 25 through July 2, the charging path sat in an Off state for 100% of eight straight days — never charging, never even logging a fault. That's equipment failure, not solar variation. Root cause (dead connection, disabled relay, power loss to the controller) is still an open question back to the hardware side.

Aug 18 – now

The forecasters were trained on the outage in progress

Both baseline models had been treating that flat eight-day zero as real solar data — and it sits right at the tail of the series, exactly where the next-step forecast is seeded from. Currently excluding the outage window from training and fixing a related mismatch (the raw log is per-minute; the lookback window assumed hourly).

02 Hawaii Island — a second site, same method

Aug 20

Second site's hardware confirmed setup

A newer, separate deployment — west coast of Hawaii Island. Same charge-controller family as Gulf Coast (Off / Fault / Bulk / Absorption / Float state model), plus a weather sensor and 24V battery. Because the state telemetry matches, the curtailment-vs-fault method built for Gulf Coast applies directly here, not just by analogy.

Aug 20 – 27

Flat daily harvest is curtailment, not weather confirmed

Daily harvest held steady at 180–240 Wh/day regardless of cloud cover swinging 26%–93% — harvest decoupled from weather is the signature of a controller curtailing once the battery hits float. Confirmed directly by the site's hardware contact: the system is oversized for its load, so it floats most days no matter the conditions.

Aug 20 – 27

Two-day power collapse traced to a mount failure confirmed

A separate near-zero power event, battery voltage sagging low, was set aside as its own question rather than folded into the curtailment story. A wind-speed uptick lined up with the timing in a side-by-side chart read — flagged as a lead, then confirmed: the panel had physically detached from its mount in high wind. Mechanical, not electrical.

Aug 27

Nameplate battery capacity is suspect open

The battery is a repurposed consumer power station (512 Wh nameplate) whose original safety electronics were damaged in an earlier, unrelated incident and are now bypassed — the cell pack runs through a generic balancer instead. The nameplate figure assumes those electronics are still in the loop, so real usable capacity needs to come from the cell specs before it feeds any safe-operating-floor math.

03 The graphs and the numbers

Headline result

LSTM: 0.93 W mean error, on the verified split

Retrained with a proper 28-day train / 7-day test split (the earlier version leaked test data into training, which is why naive baselines had shown an impossible 0.0 W error). On held-out days the LSTM's hour-ahead prediction is off by 0.93 W on average — against Prophet's 3.58 W on the same split, roughly 4× worse.

What that number is worth in practice: average solar intake on this buoy runs about 9.6 W hourly (≈230 Wh/day), against a 33.7 W camera draw. A 0.93 W error is under 10% of the power the schedule is built around — small enough that the forecast can safely front-load camera hours while the battery is full and taper through the volatile stretch, without the error itself risking a floor violation. Prophet's 3.58 W error is close to 40% of that same budget — usable for the 7-day outlook, not for hour-to-hour decisions.

Caveat carried over from Section 01: this run still trained on the Jun 25 outage window as if it were real solar data. The number itself is solid — the fix in progress is making sure it stays solid once that window is excluded.

04 Where it stands

Closed

Still open
