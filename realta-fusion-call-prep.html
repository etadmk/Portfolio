03 · How a magnetic mirror works, and why leaky is a feature

You need this well enough to follow him, not to lecture him.

Two coils create high field regions at each end. Particles moving toward a coil see a rising field and get reflected — but only if their velocity vector sits outside the loss cone. Anything scattered into the cone escapes out the end. Mirrors are inherently leaky, and that killed the concept in the 1970s.

Realta's framing, from their CSO, is that the leakiness becomes a feature rather than a hurdle: those escaping particles are charged and directional, so you can decelerate them against an electric field and collect real current. That is direct energy conversion, and it only works on open-ended machines — a tokamak has nowhere for the particles to go.

their stated power plant split: 80% of fusion power through a thermal cycle at up to 45% efficiency 20% through DEC at over 90% efficiency blended: 0.8 × 0.45 + 0.2 × 0.90 = 0.54 ~54% overall against ~45% for a pure thermal cycle

That nine-point gain is the commercial argument for DEC, and being able to do that arithmetic out loud shows you read past the headline.

04 · Where the thermal-fluids problems actually live

This is your map. A mirror puts the hard heat-flux problems in different places than a tokamak does.

The expander, and the one bit of mirror physics worth knowing

All that end-loss power has to land somewhere. The trick is magnetic expansion: as the field drops along the expander, the flux tube cross-section grows as 1/B, so the same power spreads over a much larger area. Expand from a 17 T throat down to 0.05 T at the end wall and the area grows by a factor of a few hundred, taking an impossible heat flux down to something an actively cooled surface can handle.

Worth saying: "So the end wall heat flux is set by how much magnetic expansion you can buy, and that trades against vessel size and magnet cost. Where does that land on Anvil?" That is a real question and it shows you understand the architecture rather than just the vocabulary.

Gyrotron collectors — hardware they just bought

a 1 MW gyrotron at ~50% wall-plug efficiency dumps ~1 MW into its collector spread over ~0.5 m² with beam sweeping → ~2 MW/m² = ~200 W/cm² CHF in subcooled flow boiling is the design limit

They purchased gyrotrons from Kyoto Fusioneering in March. Collector cooling is a textbook CHF-limited problem, and it is hardware arriving now rather than a 2035 concern. Naming it is very specific and very current.

05 · Your pitch — the timing argument

This is the whole call in one idea. Lead with it.

"You're fitting out the Forge and starting Hammir construction this year. A machine at that stage needs someone to build and instrument the test loops and get real data off hardware before it goes on the machine. That's what I've done for four years — boiling and evaporation rigs, high-density thermocouple arrays, flow control, degassing, DAQ, and the debugging that goes with it. I'd like to be the person who does that here."

That is a better pitch than any list of skills, because it names a need created by a decision they just made. A company standing up a new facility always has more instrumentation and commissioning work than people to do it, and it is almost never anyone's dedicated job.

Your spoken opening — about forty-five seconds

"Thanks for making time. Short version: I'm a thermal-fluids experimentalist. Two years at Illinois on pool boiling and falling-film evaporation, which turned into two papers. Then high-heat-flux capillary evaporation and CHF characterisation at UCSD. Right now I'm a DOE ORISE fellow doing power system modelling, and that wraps in two weeks. I reached out because of the Forge and Hammir timing."
Fix this before the call
Your email says you characterised CHF up to 600 W/cm². Your résumé says 600 W in one bullet and 600 W/cm² in another. Know the heated area of the GB-140 samples. A materials PhD from Intel will ask, and an undefended order of magnitude on your own data would undo everything else. Also be precise that your CHF work was capillary-fed thin-film evaporation and pool boiling, not subcooled flow boiling in channels. The methodology, instrumentation and dryout physics transfer completely — say that, rather than letting him assume flow boiling and discover otherwise.

07 · Ask him

Four at most. The first two are the ones that matter.

  • "Where does thermal-fluids sit at Realta today — is there a group, or is it distributed across the machine engineers?"
  • "What's the highest heat flux surface on Anvil, and is the plan for it settled or still open?"
  • "Fitting out the Forge — what test capability do you need that doesn't exist yet? Loops, calorimetry, thermal-hydraulic rigs?"
  • "How much expansion ratio are you buying at the end cells, and what sets the limit?"
  • "Is DEC electrode cooling solved? Cooling a surface that's at high voltage and high heat flux at the same time sounds like a genuinely awkward problem."
  • "Selling process heat means the blanket outlet temperature is effectively a product spec. Does the customer temperature requirement flow back into the blanket design?"
  • "Gyrotron collector cooling — is that coming as a package from Kyoto Fusioneering or is it yours to solve?"
  • "What surprised you when you went from Intel R&D to fusion hardware?" — if the conversation is warm, this one is a gift.

08 · Practical

  • Relocation will come up. Realta is Madison-based with hands-on lab work at UW's Physical Sciences Laboratory and soon at OM Station. Have a clear answer. Your Illinois undergrad makes the Midwest an easy story if you want it to be.
  • Say the fellowship end date. Two weeks is useful information, not a weakness — it means you are available now, which for a company mid-buildout is an advantage. State it plainly and without apology.
  • He is not the hiring manager for a role that exists. You proposed one. Expect him to ask you to define it more sharply, and expect the honest answer to be that they will need to think about where it fits. Close with "who else should I talk to?"
  • Zap. You worked at a competitor. Be normal about it: describe methods and reasoning, not proprietary compositions, and say so once up front.
